# Falsifiable Predictions

These are hypotheses to test, not established findings.

1. **Token-price decoupling**  
   Once inference is a small share of total risk-adjusted agent cost, another order-of-magnitude fall in token price should have a smaller effect on junior hiring than a comparable fall in supervision time.

2. **Supervision shock**  
   Improvements that materially reduce senior review/debug time should produce stronger labor-demand effects than benchmark gains that do not reduce supervision.

3. **Flow-before-stock**  
   AI-related labor adjustment should appear first in entry-level openings, backfill rates, and junior/senior hiring ratios before mass junior layoffs.

4. **Pipeline floor**  
   Large long-lived organizations with internal promotion pipelines should retain more graduate hiring, conditional on current task automation, than firms with low pipeline value.

5. **Regional threshold effect**  
   Regions with weaker frontier-model access may show delayed labor effects followed by rapid catch-up when local/open models cross real-task capability thresholds.

6. **Task-composition heterogeneity**  
   Standardized, verifiable implementation work should compress earlier than high-risk production ownership, architecture, security, or cross-team coordination.

7. **Potential second-stage senior compression**  
   Early effects may favor `senior + agents + fewer juniors`; later, if supervision requirements collapse, implementation-heavy mid/senior roles may also face substitution.
