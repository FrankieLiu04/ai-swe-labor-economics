# Research Ideas and Extensions

## Core idea: Senior-Attention Leverage

The potentially novel object is not merely autonomous task length or token cost, but the amount of reliable engineering execution that can be supervised by a scarce unit of experienced human attention.

Questions:

- Does SAL increase faster than METR Time Horizon?
- Is SAL primarily a model property, a harness property, or an organizational property?
- Does SAL vary more across task classes than across models?
- At what SAL does a firm's optimal junior/senior ratio change sharply?

## Expenditure Horizon reinterpretation

METR's Expenditure Horizon is best treated as a prototype of a cost-performance crossing, not a direct estimate of software labor substitution.

Potential mapping:

`benchmark capability/cost -> task success and cost parameters -> firm production function -> labor demand`

rather than:

`benchmark score -> jobs`.

## High-wage vs high-complexity tension

Higher wages increase automation incentive, but high-end jobs tend to have higher supervision, risk, and accountability requirements. This creates a non-monotonic relationship between compensation and substitution pressure.

## Graduate pipeline option value

Graduate hiring may persist even after current-period implementation value falls because firms need future internally trained senior engineers.

Possible dynamic state equation:

\[
H_{t+1}=(1-\delta)H_t+\phi J_t.
\]

## Regional phase transitions

Model access restrictions should matter most when local/open models are below a dense cluster of task thresholds. Once they cross those thresholds, employment effects can catch up quickly instead of remaining permanently delayed.

## Output-demand elasticity

Productivity does not mechanically imply lower employment. If cheaper software generates much more software demand, total engineering employment can remain stable or grow even while labor per project falls.
