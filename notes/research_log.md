# Research Log

## Stage 1 — From Time Horizon to Expenditure Horizon

The discussion began from METR's distinction between:

- **Time Horizon**: how long/complex a human task an agent can complete at a given success level.
- **Expenditure Horizon**: the budget scale up to which AI produces more task progress than human labor at equal expenditure.

Key insight: Expenditure Horizon is closer to an economic substitution concept, but it is still benchmark-specific.

## Stage 2 — Junior SWE labor-market interpretation

Initial hypothesis:

> Rising agent capability may first reduce the flow of entry-level hiring rather than trigger immediate layoffs.

The relevant labor-market signals became:

- new-grad openings;
- 0–2 YOE share;
- junior/senior posting ratio;
- backfill rate;
- young-worker hiring flows.

## Stage 3 — Segmentation

A single junior SWE market was rejected.

Provisional segments:

- T3: outsourcing / CRUD / low-complexity implementation;
- T2: median enterprise/product SWE;
- T1: large banks, exchanges, mature fintech, big-tech backend/platform/infra;
- T0: HFT/quant, frontier AI, highly complex systems.

Hypothesis: high wages increase automation incentives, while complexity/ownership increases the cost of substitution.

## Stage 4 — Regional differentiation

Regions considered:

- Mainland China;
- Hong Kong;
- Singapore;
- Tokyo/Japan.

Effective AI capability depends on both frontier access and local/open-model capability. Regional effects may therefore be threshold-like rather than a fixed permanent lag.

## Stage 5 — Wage and token-cost reasoning

The discussion highlighted two opposing corrections when mapping METR to SWE:

1. METR's human researcher cost is high relative to many Asian junior SWE wages, reducing the apparent AI cost advantage.
2. METR NanoGPT trajectories spend heavily on H100 experiments, whereas ordinary coding-agent tasks can have much lower external compute costs.

The dominant residual term may therefore become human supervision rather than inference.

## Stage 6 — Proposed new metric

**Senior-Attention Leverage (SAL)** emerged as the candidate bridge between agent benchmarks and labor demand.

Research direction:

> measure accepted human-equivalent engineering output per senior supervision hour.

## Stage 7 — Academic gap assessment

The current work is a conceptual research note, not yet an economics paper.

Major missing components:

- formal solution of the production model;
- causal identification;
- parameter calibration/estimation;
- systematic literature review;
- robust falsification and sensitivity analysis.
